0.9.0 — Public Alpha

Released 2026-10-11

  • The reconstructed three-act campaign and native Dialogic narrative become the primary game line.
  • Ten story companions, personal stories, moral choices and persistent relationships accompany the company through the Charterlands.
  • All twenty settlements have individual walkable exterior plans, moving visitors, working entrances and saved positions.
  • Secluded passages can lead to saved criminal demands, withdrawal attempts or tactical street battles.
  • Travel, discovery, regional war consequences, faction commitments, statistics and current local dialogue are included.

Start a new campaign if you are coming from the old Friends/Branch 05 build. Those saves are intentionally unsupported and must stay with their old game. Current Branch 07 campaigns remain supported; back them up before updating.

Cinder Abbey

  • The abbey's gate is a card, not the battle map: the painting, who is here, and three choices: speak to Mara, enter the abbey grounds, or stay on the road. It is shown on every visit until the abbey is settled. Speaking to Mara goes in with her words already on the screen; entering begins nothing. The main story is never forced.
  • Paying for passage now shows what the company earned at once. The banner used to wait for a change into and out of combat, which a bought passage never makes, so it appeared on the next visit instead. It now fires when any ground becomes settled, by arms or by terms (titled Settled for terms), and never again on coming back.
  • Once settled, the abbey says so plainly: Back to the map, Gather the spoils, and an objective line to match. The old Return to camp and Recover spoils / Equipment labels are gone.
  • Figures no longer clip through pillars and walls. They were flat planes that faced the camera fully, so they leaned back through whatever stood behind them and forward through whatever stood in front. They now stand upright on their hex and turn only about the vertical, at the same size on screen.

Buttons

  • A button standard for the whole game, applied where every button is made: one lettering size (15, or 18 on tall buttons), a least height of 38, the same clear space before, after, above and below the words, words that step down a size at a time when a button is narrow (never below 12), and a tooltip that carries any words that still had to be clipped.
  • The battle screen's Save, Gear, Menu and More, its action bar, and the map's bottom bar are re-laid to the standard; buttons that forced a smaller font no longer do.

Journal and finding the story

  • Main quests in the journal now read: What to do next, Where (with how to get there, or that the company is already there), and How (what to press and what to expect), in plain words for any age.

  • Response time: The first line is one short request shaped by regard and shown at once; options follow in a second small request. First lines and options for the proprietor and up to two visitors are prepared while the venue screen is open, so approaching a prepared person is immediate. While the player reads, the reply to every visible option is prepared and checked in parallel; choosing a prepared option answers at once. A retry is offered when a reply cannot be prepared.
  • Boundaries and checks: A narrow deterministic gate blocks unsafe text, any gift, sale, loan, reward, service or quest offer, and claims about the visitor beyond what the sheet says they can see; one model review checks turn replies for commitments, unannounced events and visitor claims. Opinion changes remain game-owned weights on the chosen tone. Sheets, happenings, sessions and the ledger are validated on load; an Inspect sheet button shows the record during testing.
