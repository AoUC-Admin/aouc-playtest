History before the company

From answering-irons to the empty throne.

Shared history and the opening situation are described here. Campaign revelations, solutions and endings remain for players to discover.

The Age of Answering

Long before the present war, answering-irons bound the dead to the demands of the living. Death did not reliably end a person’s service. The memory of that age gives the return of coerced dead its horror: the threat is not only destruction, but labor and obedience without an ending.

Oran and the Long Vigil

The old histories tell of Oran the Wakeful, the Long Vigil and five Hearth-Oaths that protected the dead from renewed compulsion. Their legacy belongs to the shared past of the realms. What people remember, believe and understand about those protections is not always the same thing.

The imperial order

Five crowns retained their own lands and traditions within a wider imperial order. Roads, passes, rivers and sea lanes bound their fortunes together. An imperial seat did not erase local loyalties, old grievances or the power of guilds and assemblies.

The Uncrowning

The Human king Hadran conspired in the murder of the Elven emperor Ithariel. Hadran’s crown was broken and he died in immediate retaliation. The collapse was recent: the empty Human throne, unanswered claims and military intervention shape the world the player enters.

The present crisis

Elven forces have entered the Charterlands. Arms interests profit from both sides, local authorities compete to hold their communities together, and Serevath’s use of the dead threatens something more lasting than a change of rulers. Bellwether’s missing people, debts and frightened witnesses make these pressures personal before the company understands their full reach.
